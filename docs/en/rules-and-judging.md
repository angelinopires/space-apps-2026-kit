## Team rules

- 1 to 6 members. Universal Event teams need at least 2.
- Everyone on the team must be registered for the 2026 hackathon, be on the **same** Local Event (not waitlisted) and appear on the team's Members tab. No new members after submission closes.
- One team, one challenge. You can only be on one team.
- Under 18: registered by a parent or guardian; no photos, video, voice or likeness of anyone under 18 in the avatar, team page or project.

## Eligibility for Global Judging (minimum requirements)

1. Respond to one of the 14 official challenges. "Create your own challenge" projects are not eligible for Global Judging.
2. Fill every required field on the Project Submission page.
3. Provide a **demo**: a slide deck of **at most 7 slides** (title and intro slides count) **or** a video of **at most 30 seconds**. Over the limit means not eligible.
4. **English** on the submission page and in all linked materials. A demo video may be spoken in another language **with English subtitles**. Slide decks must be in English.
5. Submit before the deadline above.
6. Tick the two boxes: you read the submission requirements and accept the Terms; and the work is original and every external resource (code, text, images, even open source) is listed in the References field, with no copyrighted material you lack permission for.
7. NASA's China funding restriction review applies (see the Terms).

## What goes on the Project Submission page

The page is what judges read. Fields (as of the 2026 guide):

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Project name | Short. Can say what problem it solves or what people gain |
| High-level project summary | Elevator pitch: what you built, how it addresses the challenge, why it matters |
| Link to project demo | Public URL (YouTube, Drive, GitHub, OneDrive, Dropbox). 7 slides or 30 s. No login, no permissions |
| Link to final project | Public URL to the running app, repo, or files. If judges cannot open it, it is not judged |
| Detailed project description | What it does, how it works, benefits, what you hope to achieve, tools/languages/hardware |
| NASA data | Which NASA data or resources you used and **how**. At least one NASA data source or resource is required to be eligible for a Global Award. Judges score Relevance on this |
| Space Agency Partner and other data | Partner-agency data and every other resource |
| Use of AI | Whether you used AI tools and how. Data-collection only, judges do not see the answer, but they do see watermarks and notes in the project |
| References | Every dataset, library, image, text, code you used, even open source. Required |

Practical rules from the guide:
- Images (JPG, PNG, GIF up to 10 MB) can be pasted into the page. Videos and documents cannot: host them elsewhere and link.
- Test every link in a private browser window before submitting.
- Save often. Use the edit **Lock** toggle so teammates do not overwrite each other.
- If your project is a new website, you may request a [FortiGuard web filter classification](https://www.fortiguard.com/faq/wfratingsubmit) so judges' networks do not block it (Company Name: "N/A").

## Use of AI (the guide calls it "SI")

- Allowed and encouraged as a tool to accelerate building. It should not be what comes up with your idea of how to solve the challenge.
- Generated content must **not** use or modify NASA branding (logos, flags, mission identifiers).
- Mark where AI was used: images and video need a **visible watermark**; audio, code and data need an acknowledgment in descriptive text and metadata.
- Judges evaluate originality, intent and execution of the AI-assisted parts you disclose.

## Judging criteria

Every level of judging scores 1 to 5 on each of five criteria. Equal weight. The text below is a close paraphrase of the official definitions.

| Criterion | What a 5 looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Impact** | Addresses a significant problem; wide reach; inspires or benefits a large audience; quality far exceeds expectations |
| **Creativity** | Original, novel approach or a meaningful improvement on existing ideas; challenges conventional methods |
| **Validity** | Scientifically sound and technically feasible; fully developed and ready for real use; usable and user-friendly |
| **Relevance** | Fully and directly addresses the chosen challenge; **NASA data is integral**, not a superficial add-on, and drives the insights |
| **Presentation** | Clearly conveys challenge, solution and significance; engaging, well-structured storytelling; judges use the demo **and** the full project page |

The guide's own advice: teams tend to work hard on the first four and neglect Presentation. Give the pitch and the story real time, and put someone on design and storytelling.

## Judging phases

1. **Local judging** (after the hackathon): judges recruited by your Local Lead score every submission from that event and pick the **Global Nominees**. Local awards are separate and optional.
2. **Global judging**: expert judges review all Global Nominees and pick the **Global Finalists** and **Honorable Mentions**.
3. **Executive judging**: NASA and Space Agency Partner executives pick the ten **Global Winners**.

## The ten Global Awards

- Best Use of Science
- Best Use of Data
- Best Use of Technology
- Galactic Impact
- Best Mission Concept
- Most Inspirational
- Best Storytelling Award
- Global Connection Award
- Art & Technology Award
- Local Impact Award

Pick one you can plausibly win and shape the story toward it.
